The gate — Collection and Recovery are one decision, not two

Remanufacturing without regional triage loses a third of its yield before the line starts, because cores arrive aged and multiply handled. Adding the two levers' separate values overstates the case badly.

The disposition cascade

Of every 100 units shipped, 20 come back — the brief's own return rate. Each is graded against five questions in order. Recycling is the residual, not the default, and that inversion of sequence is the whole mechanism.

What each rung is worth

Value retained and manufacturing footprint avoided, both expressed against one new product = 1.00. Processing intensity is on the same relative scale.

A resold unit displaces almost an entire new build; a recycled one displaces very little of it. That spread — not the cost of the reverse network — is what makes the Recovery decision worth taking seriously.

The same 20 units under each network

Network A asks no questions at all: a unit with a scuffed carton and a unit with a dead board go on the same truck and are shredded together.

Cost by block — Network A against your configuration

Every block as a share of Network A's total network cost. The mix itself is set to match the prevalent structure of a distribution network: transport around 55%, facilities around 25%, handling and processing around 12%, packaging around 8%.

Carbon by block

Operating carbon only. Avoided manufacturing carbon — the footprint of the new units that never had to be built — is reported separately below.

The net ledger

Cost, recovered value and service loss on one currency: the value unit, where 1.00 is the worth of one new product. Network A's whole network cost is anchored at 10 value units — the prevalent norm that supply-chain cost runs at about a tenth of sales.

What each lever is worth

Net-cost index points saved. Measured three ways: the lever alone, the lever inside the full network, and the lever engineered. Watch Transport.

VistaraTech Network Designer — companion application to the 16-slide submission “Designing the Sustainable Supply Chain Network”. Raghavendar R · MBA Operations Management 2025-27 · Symbiosis Institute of Operations Management.
VistaraTech is a fictional company defined in the course case brief. This is a closed-world model: every structural fact comes from the brief, and every parameter is a ratio drawn from prevalent industry practice. It contains no prices, no company financials and no external emissions dataset, so all cost and carbon results are indices with Network A = 100 rather than currency or tonnes. The benchmark evidence cited in the deck is used only to characterise what other companies have built, never to parameterise this model.
